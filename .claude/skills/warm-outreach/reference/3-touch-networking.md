# Reference: The 3-Touch Networking System

**Source:** David Fano (Founder/CEO, Teal), LinkedIn post, ~March 2026.
https://www.linkedin.com/posts/davidfano_the-3-touch-networking-system-activity-7431700801858957312-nF_v

**Status: optional reference, not a required workflow.** Use it when the user is building a
relationship over weeks with someone they have no tie to yet. The default warm-outreach flow
(NU alumni first, one well-aimed connection note) is usually faster and works fine on its own.
Do not run all three touches on someone who already accepted and replied warmly; that is when
you just talk to them like a person.

**Verify before citing:** the post opens with "70% of jobs are filled through networking."
That figure gets repeated constantly online but has no solid primary source behind it.
Never put it in a document, application, or message for the user without checking it first.

---

## The three touches

### Touch 1: Connect with a personalized note
No default connection request. Reference something specific about the person. Under 300
characters (LinkedIn's limit anyway). Make it about them, not about the user.

Shape:
> "Hi [Name], I saw your post about [topic] and your point about [specific insight] stuck with
> me. I'm exploring [industry/role type] and would love to connect."

### Touch 2: Add value, ask for nothing
Wait 3 to 7 days after they accept. Then give something with no ask attached. Options:

- Share something relevant to their work, with a line on why it made you think of them.
- Congratulate a real milestone (promotion, launch, company news).
- Leave a thoughtful comment on their post. No self-pitch.

Goal is to be someone helpful on their radar, not someone waiting to cash in.

### Touch 3: Ask for insight, not a job
Only after touch 2. Ask a tight question they can answer in two minutes.

Shape:
> "I'm targeting [role type] roles and would value 10 minutes of your perspective. What's one
> thing you wish you'd known before moving into [their role]?"

> "I'm sharpening my approach to [specific skill]. Based on your work at [Company], what would
> you prioritize learning?"

The distinction that matters: asking someone to share what they know, not to hand over a job.

## The best parts (from the comment thread)

Margaret Buj (talent acquisition lead) pushed back usefully, and Fano agreed:

- **Pacing and intent beat the template.** If every touch visibly points at an ask, it still
  reads as transactional. If the person can tell a playbook is being run on them, trust breaks.
  Treat it as long-term relationship building.
- **"Add value" rarely means sending an article.** A thoughtful comment on their work, or an
  observation from your own experience, shows you're actually near their world. A link is the
  weakest version of this.
- **Specificity is what earns replies.** "What's one thing you'd prioritize learning in X right
  now" lands. "Can I pick your brain" does not.

## How this fits the user's existing rules

It agrees with what's already in `CLAUDE.md` (open with something specific, lead with proof,
keep the ask small and time-boxed) and adds two things worth keeping:

1. **The 3-to-7 day gap** between acceptance and the next message. The user's current flow drafts an
   optional follow-up but doesn't specify timing.
2. **A no-ask middle touch.** Useful when the first note lands but there's no natural reason to
   ask for anything yet.

Where the user is stronger than the template: they have real proof to lead with (published research,
national titles, shipped builds). The generic version of touch 1 is weaker than an opener that
names something they actually did or saw firsthand. Prefer their own material over the template
wording.
